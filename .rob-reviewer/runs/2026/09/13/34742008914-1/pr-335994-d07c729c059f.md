## Performance regression fixes detected (1)

### 1. concurrency-burst

**Fix location:** `build/azure-pipelines/darwin/codesign.ts:14` (`RIGHT`)  
**Blamed base line:** `build/azure-pipelines/darwin/codesign.ts:51`  
**Introducing path:** `build/azure-pipelines/darwin/codesign.ts`  
**Introducing commit:** `ed8f9653dadc3029f14bf71917bee5d335c20732`  
**Mechanism family:** `concurrency-burst`  
**Performance category:** `subprocess`  
**Confidence:** 0.96

**Mechanism:** A global phase barrier serialized the start of every ESRP notarization behind completion of the slowest signing subprocess, leaving already-signed artifacts idle and making step latency max(all signing durations) plus max(all notarization durations) rather than the maximum per-artifact sign/notarize chain.

**Symptom:** Official macOS build completion was delayed whenever artifact signing durations differed, because notarization for early-finished artifacts could not overlap the remaining signing work.

**Fixed behavior:** Each artifact now forms an independent sign-then-notarize chain, so a completed artifact enters the external notarization operation immediately instead of idling behind the slowest unrelated signing job. The total boundary-call count remains four operations for universal builds or eight for architecture-specific builds, and effective concurrency remains bounded by the two or four artifact chains.

**Evidence:** In the base revision, all 2 (universal) or 4 (architecture-specific) sign-darwin subprocesses were started, then every signing result was awaited through line 47 before the first notarize-darwin subprocess could be spawned at line 51. Each subprocess invokes the synchronous dotnet ESRP CLI path in build/azure-pipelines/common/sign.ts, and the encompassing Azure Pipelines Codesign & Notarize step cannot complete until all operations finish. The changed helper awaits only the matching artifact's signing operation before starting its notarization, and Promise.all retains parallel processing across independent artifacts.

### Scenario 3: Signing or notarization failure during an official macOS build

- Critical path: Promise.all rejects when the first active chain reports failure and main exits the process. Other child processes are terminated with the parent; unlike the old global barrier, a fast successful sign may have begun its own notarization before a different sign fails.
- Scaling input: Bounded fixed set of two or four artifact chains, not an unbounded collection or recurring event.
- Effective concurrency: Still bounded by two or four active chains. Fail-fast rejection can shorten failure reporting; successful sibling chains may transiently overlap later-stage work.
- Cache behavior: No retry cache or shared result cache is visible; ESRP retry behavior is unchanged. Failure does not populate reusable state in this script.
- Mechanism confidence: High for possible early sibling notarization launch; low for any material residual resource cost after fail-fast process exit.
- Magnitude uncertainty: Whether an ESRP request continues remotely after parent termination is not established, and failed official builds are not the primary successful-build scenario.
- Expensive boundaries:
  - subprocess at <code>build/azure-pipelines/darwin/codesign.ts:11-14,43-49</code>: Sibling chains may have started notarization before another signing subprocess reports failure.; cardinality: At most one live subprocess per artifact chain; on a failed run, up to one notarization per already-signed sibling can start earlier than under the prior barrier.
    - Introduced by diff: true; previous behavior: No notarization started until every signing await succeeded, so a signing failure prevented the notarization phase.; critical-path effect: The first rejection ends the pipeline step rather than awaiting all siblings, so this work does not extend the failed step's foreground critical path.
  - network at <code>build/azure-pipelines/common/sign.ts:110-116,166-216</code>: An ESRP notarization request for an already-signed sibling may be submitted before another signing request fails.; cardinality: Bounded by the other one or three artifact chains and only occurs in failed builds; no increase on successful builds.
    - Introduced by diff: true; previous behavior: A sign-phase failure prevented all notarization requests.; critical-path effect: The process exits on the first rejection; any remote residual work is not awaited and has no evidenced user-visible latency impact.
- Verdict: No reportable regression. The extra failure-only work is tightly bounded, does not increase peak concurrency, and lacks evidence of material latency or resource impact; fail-fast behavior may instead reduce failed-build duration.
