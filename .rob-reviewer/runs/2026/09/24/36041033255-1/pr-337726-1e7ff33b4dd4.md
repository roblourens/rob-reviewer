## Findings (1)

### 1. [medium] Cancel sibling renderers after the first shard failure

**Finding ID:** `PERF-E49492716316`  
**Location:** <code>test/componentFixtures/renderFixtures.mts:128</code> (<code>RIGHT</code>)  
**Performance category:** <code>subprocess</code>  
**Mechanism family:** <code>cleanup-lifecycle</code>  
**Resource:** A component-explorer Node process and its transitive npm/rspack dev server and browser processes, including their CPU and memory usage.  
**Scaling:** The wasted work grows with the number and cost of fixtures assigned to the surviving shard; an immediate failure in one shard leaves nearly a complete half-suite render running.  
**Outcome:** Delayed failed-workflow latency and wasted CI CPU/memory/subprocess work.  
**PR causality:** <code>introduced</code>  
**Previous behavior:** The workflow invoked one component-explorer renderer directly, so a renderer startup or process failure ended the screenshot command without an independent full render continuing on the critical path.  
**Changed behavior:** The new coordinator starts two renderer subprocesses and awaits them with Promise.allSettled, which cannot return until both subprocesses close even when one has already rejected.  
**Confidence:** 0.93 — The two-process call path and allSettled wait semantics are explicit in the changed file, as is the absence of cancellation ownership. The exact frequency of early process failures is uncertain, which limits severity, but the full-shard cost whenever this trigger occurs is deterministic.

**Causal diff evidence:** RIGHT line 128 introduces Promise.allSettled over both newly spawned render shards; the prior workflow line invoked only one renderer and had no sibling process to await after failure.

If one shard fails early (for example during server/browser startup or from a shard-specific renderer crash), the failed workflow still waits while the other shard renders its entire share of the fixture suite. This delays CI failure feedback by up to the surviving shard's render time and burns runner CPU, memory, and subprocess capacity even though no merged output can be produced.

**Evidence:** fixtureShards contains two complementary patterns; renderShard spawns a component-explorer CLI process for each, and each CLI reaches the configured npm/rspack server plus browser rendering path. Promise.allSettled at line 128 deliberately waits for both renderShard promises, and no child handle, AbortSignal, or process-tree termination is retained to stop the surviving shard after the first rejection.

**Suggested direction:** Retain cancellation ownership for both child process trees and, on the first shard rejection, terminate the remaining renderer/server/browser tree, await child cleanup, then throw the collected failure. Preserve the current all-success merge and ensure cancellation does not leave descendants running.

- Effective concurrency: Two shards start concurrently. After one fails, effective useful concurrency becomes zero, but the sibling subprocess tree continues at one-shard concurrency with no cancellation or backpressure until completion.
- Cache behavior: No failed-result cache or cancellation coalescing exists. The surviving shard continues its already-started cold rspack/server/browser lifecycle and fixture rendering even though its output cannot be merged.
