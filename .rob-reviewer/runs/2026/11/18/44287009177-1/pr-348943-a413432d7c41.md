### Scenario 1: Create a Copilot session, accumulate ordinary and child-turn correlation history, then cancel or dispose it.

- Critical path: Session construction adds an empty LRU; starting a root turn adds an empty Set and scalar state synchronously. Recording interaction evidence performs in-memory lookups and bounded insertion, without starting provider, database, filesystem, or network work.
- Scaling input: Live SDK sessions, observed execution-turn IDs, and distinct interactions per turn. Each session retains at most 1,000 correlation entries and 64 interaction IDs per entry; unresolved steering can additionally retain a predecessor snapshot of at most 64 IDs.
- Effective concurrency: State mutations are synchronous on the host JavaScript thread. The diff adds no task fan-out, timers, subprocesses, or background initialization. Existing active execution-routing Sets are still cleared with CopilotTurn disposal.
- Cache behavior: The LRU starts empty, creates entries on startTurn or an exact response association, deduplicates IDs with Set.has, marks overflow without retaining additional IDs, and evicts old turns. Missing reads do not trigger recovery I/O. Registered disposal clears the LRU. Predecessor contexts contain scalar IDs and flat arrays, not recursive links to turn objects.
- Mechanism confidence: High: inspected the complete new correlation implementation, LRU trimming, constructor disposal registration, CopilotTurn disposal, and session super.dispose path.
- Magnitude uncertainty: Actual live-session counts and interaction counts are unmeasured; the retained history is explicitly bounded rather than proportional to the entire session lifetime.
- Expensive boundaries: none found
- Verdict: No actionable retention, eager-initialization, or cleanup regression established. The added bounded analytical history does not extend the lifetime of active execution-routing objects.
