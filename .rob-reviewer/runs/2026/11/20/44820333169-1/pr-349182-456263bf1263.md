# Performance review: editor: place the cursor on clicks between the folding chevron and the text

[PR #349182](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/pull/349182) at `456263bf1263bce405de191b183ca1fdc46f00a8`

## Findings (1)

### 1. [medium] Avoid rebuilding line decorations on every margin mousemove

**Finding ID:** `PERF-15D2A7DFB178`  
**Location:** <code>src/vs/editor/browser/controller/mouseTarget.ts:730</code> (<code>RIGHT</code>)  
**Performance category:** <code>cpu</code>  
**Mechanism family:** <code>repeated-work</code>  
**Resource:** Bracket/decorations enumeration, recreated model-decoration/options objects, result arrays, and InlineDecoration objects.  
**Scaling:** For M delivered mousemoves over the same eligible gutter area and D decorations intersecting the queried line, the added processing and allocation are O(M × D), including on warm caches. The unwrapped-line case queries the whole line; there is no per-line result coalescing or mousemove throttling in this path.  
**Outcome:** Additional synchronous renderer CPU work and temporary allocations on every qualifying mousemove can cause input latency and frame drops on decoration-dense lines.  
**PR causality:** <code>introduced</code>  
**Previous behavior:** The same gutter movements returned GUTTER\_LINE\_DECORATIONS after the existing position/range calculation. They did not call getTextDirection, enumerate bracket decorations, or rebuild inline-decoration collections.  
**Changed behavior:** Ordinary movement over the empty line-decoration gutter now synchronously calls getTextDirection, which collects and processes all decorations for the view line before returning the mouse target.  
**Confidence:** 0.97 — The native mousemove listener, new branch, uncached getDecorationsOnLine call, and per-decoration reconstruction are directly traced. Bracket colorization provides a concrete collection-scaling trigger even with warm render caches; the duration on a particular machine is not measured.

**Causal diff evidence:** The added call to ctx.isRtl(lineNumber) at mouseTarget.ts:730 runs in the new decoration-free margin branch. Previously \_hitTestMargin returned fulfillMargin directly without querying text direction or collecting line decorations. MouseHandler.\_onMouseMove invokes this hit test for every delivered mousemove, including repeated movements over the same line.

With an unwrapped generated/minified JSON or source line containing thousands of colored brackets, moving the pointer through the decoration-free gutter repeatedly walks those brackets and rebuilds decoration arrays on the renderer thread, increasing CPU/GC pressure and delaying input and painting even when the document is unchanged.

**Evidence:** HitTestContext.isRtl delegates to ViewModel.getTextDirection (mouseTarget.ts:370-371). ViewModelImpl.getTextDirection calls \_decorations.getDecorationsOnLine and then scans the result (viewModelImpl.ts:859-880). getDecorationsOnLine bypasses the viewport cache and calls InlineModelDecorationsComputer.getDecorations every time (viewModelDecorations.ts:69-81); that method re-queries the model, builds result arrays, and creates InlineDecoration objects (inlineDecorations.ts:92-164). TextModel.getDecorationsInRange also invokes ColorizedBracketPairsDecorationProvider, which recreates decoration/options objects for every bracket in the range (textModel.ts:1826-1832; colorizedBracketPairsDecorationProvider.ts:45-69). The existing conversion cache does not eliminate these enumerations or allocations.

**Suggested direction:** Avoid recomputing direction from the full decoration collection for each mousemove. Expose/reuse the rendered line's already-computed direction (ViewLine.isRenderedRTL exists), or cache the authoritative direction in the view-model lifecycle with invalidation on decoration and line-mapping changes.
